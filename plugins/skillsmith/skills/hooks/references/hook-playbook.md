# Hook playbook

A hook earns the next three seconds. It does that with a specific fact, a
tension, or a picture the reader recognises. It never does it with adjectives.

## Hook shapes that work

| Shape | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number with a consequence | [number] + what it means for the reader | "I paid for 3 trials I forgot to cancel last year. That's 47 dollars for nothing." |
| Before / after | the old way, then the new way, both concrete | "My trials used to live in 6 calendar notes. Now they live in one list that warns me." |
| Confession | the author admits a mistake or a cost | "I trusted 'all tests pass' for a year. They didn't." |
| Enemy | name what the reader is fighting | "Free trials are designed for you to forget them." |
| Question with a sting | a question the reader cannot answer comfortably | "How many subscriptions are you paying for right now? Without checking." |
| Proof first | show the result, then explain | "This tiny site saved me 12 dollars on day one." |
| Counter-intuitive | a true fact that sounds wrong | "Asking the AI not to cheat changed nothing: 80% before, 80% after." |

## Numbers

- Every number in a hook carries a tag: `[S#]` for research, `[P]` for a fact
  about the product that anyone can check.
- Use the number as found. "30.4%" stays "30.4%" or becomes "about a third",
  never "nearly half".
- A number with no source is cut, however good it sounds.

## Cut without mercy

- Anything a competitor could also say word for word.
- Adjectives doing the job of facts: amazing, powerful, seamless, innovative.
- Openers that delay: "In today's world", "Have you ever wondered".
- Fake suspense: "The result? ...", "Here's the thing."
- "Not just X, it's Y."
- Rockets, sparkles and fire emoji.
- More than one exclamation mark per post.
- In English copy, em dashes used as all-purpose glue.

Put the strongest cut lines in the kill list with the reason. Wrap that list
in `<!-- ss:slop-ignore -->` markers so the detector skips it.

## Platforms

Check the platform's current rules before posting; they change. If the
product talks to people through AI, the copy says so: from 2 August 2026 the
EU AI Act (Article 50) requires telling people they are interacting with an
AI system.

| Platform | Format notes |
|---|---|
| Reddit | Title carries the hook. Body: the story and the mechanics first, link last. Read the subreddit rules on self-promotion. Answer every comment in the first hour. |
| X | First post stands alone. One idea per post. Image or video in the first or second post. |
| YouTube | Title and thumbnail are one unit. Say the hook in the first 10 seconds and show it on screen. |
| TikTok / Reels / Shorts | Vertical 9:16. The hook is spoken and written on screen in the first second. Captions on. |
| Instagram | First line before "more" is the hook. Carousel slide 1 is the hook. |
| Threads | Conversational first line; one idea per post; reply to your own post with the link. |
| Hacker News | "Show HN:" title states what it is, plainly. No marketing words. Be in the comments to answer technical questions. |
| Email | Subject line is the hook; first sentence earns the second; one link, one ask. |
| LinkedIn | First two lines before "see more". Story over announcement. |
| Product Hunt | Tagline under 60 characters. First comment from the maker tells the story. |

## Inside the product

The same discipline applies to the words in the product:

- A headline says what the visitor gets, in their words.
- A button says exactly what happens: "Add trial", not "Submit".
- An empty screen invites action: "No trials yet. Add the one you signed up for today."
- An error says what went wrong and how to fix it, without apologising.
